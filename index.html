1. Question


Title: Predictive Content Opportunity Scoring: A Machine Learning Approach to SEO Refresh Prioritization

Abstract:
Content and SEO teams often struggle to identify which decaying pages offer the best return on investment for a manual refresh. To solve this, this study utilizes search performance data to build a predictive opportunity scoring engine. A Random Forest model was trained to score content potential, utilizing a strict GroupShuffleSplit methodology to prevent data leakage. Finally, the model's outputs are translated into a ranked action playbook, mapping scores to specific human-in-the-loop recommendations.

The Research Question:
How can we identify and rank decaying web pages that offer the highest return on investment for manual content refreshes?

The Decision it Supports:
This research supports content strategists and SEO teams in prioritizing their update queues. Instead of relying on subjective guesswork, teams can use this data-driven scoring engine to allocate resources efficiently towards pages that have the highest potential for recovery.

3. Methodology

Assumptions & Definitions:

Features: The model utilizes gsc_impressions and gsc_avg_position as the primary indicators of a page's current visibility and potential.

Target Label: gsc_clicks is used as the target variable to represent the realized value (opportunity score proxy).

Model: A RandomForestRegressor was selected to capture non-linear relationships between position, impressions, and expected clicks.

Validation Design & Leakage Checks:
Initially, a standard random train/test split was used as a baseline. However, this approach is fundamentally flawed for multi-client data, as pages from the same client can leak into both the training and testing sets, leading to over-optimistic results. To ensure an honest evaluation, a strict GroupShuffleSplit was implemented, grouped by client_hash_id. This guarantees that the model is tested on entirely unseen clients.

Furthermore, a leakage audit was conducted between impressions and clicks. While there is a strong synchronous correlation (impressions limit the maximum possible clicks), this overlap is acceptable because the model functions as a directional ranking tool for existing pages, not a strict forecaster of future traffic.

5. Limitations


Honest Claims & Boundaries:

Correlation vs. Causation: This model identifies correlations between visibility metrics and click opportunities. A high opportunity score suggests potential, but it does not guarantee that a manual refresh will causally result in increased traffic.

Semantic Blindness: The model analyzes quantitative metrics (impressions, positions), not qualitative content. It cannot determine if a page is struggling due to poor writing quality, outdated facts, or external factors like a lack of backlinks.

Algorithm Volatility: The system is built on historical trailing data (February 2026). It cannot predict or account for sudden search engine algorithm updates or real-time viral trends.

6. Ranked recommendations


The Action Playbook:
Based on the opportunity score and traffic decay signals, the model categorizes URLs into the following prioritized actions:

Full Rewrite: (High Opportunity + Active Decay) Content needs a complete structural and semantic overhaul.

Expand Content: (High Opportunity + Stable Traffic) Content is performing reasonably well but lacks the depth to capture available clicks.

Refresh Links/Dates: (Medium Opportunity + Minor Decay) Quick SEO wins requiring minimal effort.

Monitor: (Low Opportunity) Low priority; not worth manual resource allocation at this time.

Human-in-the-Loop Rules:
This system is a prioritization engine, not a content generator. All "Rewrite" or "Expand" recommendations must be reviewed by a human content strategist. Automated, direct-to-site overwriting using LLMs is strictly prohibited.
